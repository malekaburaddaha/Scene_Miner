# Milestone 12 — figures and the CVPR paper

## Figures (`paper/figures/`)

All three are generated by `scripts/make_figures.py`, which reads every number
from the Milestone 1–11 result JSONs. Nothing is hand-transcribed, so a figure
cannot drift from the numbers it is drawn from.

| file | content |
|---|---|
| `fig1_architecture.pdf` | Two-stage architecture: frozen Stage 1 → cached signals → Stage 2 (PartNorm, routed base under an entropy floor, zero-init gated residual), with the monocular path called out. |
| `fig2_qualitative.pdf` | Top retrievals for three queries across nuScenes/Waymo (602-scene eval split) and BDD100K, the model applied unchanged to the external monocular corpus. |
| `fig3_audit.pdf` | Two-panel audit: (a) benchmark circularity, the attribute index at 1.000 by construction; (b) detector contamination, +0.3349 mAP between seen and unseen scenes. |

Each figure was rendered and visually inspected, not merely compiled. Three
rounds of layout fixes were applied: overlapping annotations in fig1, colliding
tick labels and a literal `\%` escape in fig3, and overflowing row labels and
colliding column titles in fig2.

## Paper (`paper/main_cvpr.tex`)

Compiles to **6 pages** with `pdflatex`, no errors and no overfull boxes. It
uses `article.cls` with an `\IfFileExists` switch that picks up the official
`cvpr.sty` author kit automatically when that is dropped into the directory
(the kit is not in this repository).

### Structure

The paper is organised as the user specified — three Audit Discoveries plus the
v2.2 method — with a fourth section that tests the premise:

1. **Audit Discovery 1 — the benchmark is circular.** Relevance is a predicate
   over cached VLM attributes, so an attribute index scores exactly 1.000 mAP /
   0.992 P@5 / 1.000 NDCG@10. The VLM's precision against 550 human-labelled
   scenes is 0.50–0.68, so the benchmark rewards reproducing its errors.
2. **Audit Discovery 2 — the certifying detector is contaminated.** 0.7864 mAP
   on the 278 scenes it trained on versus 0.4514 on 150 unseen val scenes:
   +0.3349, +74 %. Includes the footnote on the hand-rolled matcher that
   inverted the night/day signal (r = +0.96) before the official protocol was
   used.
3. **Audit Discovery 3 — routing collapse, and what actually caused the
   transfer failure.** The collapse (0.980 → 0.535 mean margin weight), the
   finding that repairing it alone moved BDD100K by +0.0004, and the real
   mechanism: a convex router over parts differing by 28.1× in scale, which is
   how a blend scored below both of its inputs.
4. **SceneMiner v2.2** — the five mechanisms, with the measured latency
   (5.91 ± 0.02 ms to score all 1508 scenes for one query on an A40).
5. **Results** — human GT, BDD100K, in-domain ablations, domain skew.
6. **Does the premise hold?** — the targeted CAM_FRONT test.

### Every non-reproducing claim replaced

The WACV draft's claims that did not reproduce have been removed entirely rather
than softened. None of the following appears in `main_cvpr.tex`:

| WACV claim | status | replaced by |
|---|---|---|
| Table 1 magnitudes (erank 45.6→72.0, routing risk 0.117→0.137, span 7.35→9.96) | does not reproduce | the measured routing table, 0.980 → 0.535 |
| §4.2 τ drift below 1.0, clamp binding | does not reproduce (τ rises: 1.070 at 3k, 1.249 at 12k) | dropped; no τ claim is made |
| §4.3 BLIP-2 token collapse (0/32 tokens below 1 % mass) | does not reproduce | dropped |
| erank(L) = 47.6 | unreachable (matrix rank 38 < 48) | dropped |
| §6.8 downstream utility | does not reproduce on uncontaminated val150 | replaced by the targeted CAM_FRONT test, which does reproduce |

### What the paper explicitly does not claim

A dedicated Discussion paragraph states the negatives rather than burying them:
that SceneMiner does **not** beat zero-shot SigLIP2 on BDD100K (0.5952 vs
0.6218); that de-shortcutting is **not** free (−0.029 mAP in domain, glare FN
recovery 7/20 → 1/20); and that the skew figures do **not** resolve two-point
differences at n = 150 queries. The Limitations paragraph discloses the single
rater, the single monocular external corpus, and the one design choice (text
mean-centring) informed by that external corpus.
